# Amazon Web Services (AWS) in Universities

## 1. Introduction

Amazon Web Services (AWS) is a cloud computing platform operated by Amazon that provides organisations with on-demand access to computing power, storage, databases, networking, artificial intelligence, machine learning and many other digital services through the internet. Instead of an organisation purchasing, installing and maintaining large amounts of physical computer hardware, AWS allows it to access computing resources when they are required and pay according to the resources used (Amazon Web Services, n.d.).

Cloud computing is particularly relevant to universities because higher education institutions depend on information technology for teaching, learning, research and administration. Universities operate systems such as student registration platforms, learning management systems (LMS), student information systems, examination systems, email services and research databases. These systems can experience significant increases in usage during periods such as registration, examinations and the release of results. AWS can provide universities with the ability to increase or decrease computing resources according to demand.

The South African university environment makes cloud computing particularly relevant. Universities must manage limited financial resources, unreliable electricity supply in some circumstances, connectivity challenges and increasing demand for digital education. Cloud services can help institutions reduce their dependence on physical infrastructure while providing access to modern computing technologies. However, universities must also consider security, data protection, cost management, connectivity and staff skills before moving important systems to the cloud.

This assignment examines AWS and its relevance to universities, provides background on cloud computing and AWS in higher education, discusses the benefits of AWS for South African universities, examines the challenges associated with adoption, and provides recommendations for universities considering or expanding their use of AWS.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Development of Cloud Computing

Cloud computing developed from earlier forms of shared computing. During the 1950s and 1960s, mainframe computers were expensive and organisations used time-sharing systems to allow multiple users to share computing resources. This introduced the idea that computing power could be shared rather than every user or organisation needing to own an individual computer system (Cloud Revolution, n.d.).

As computer networks and internet technologies developed, organisations increasingly became able to access computing resources remotely. Modern cloud computing developed from these earlier technologies by allowing users to access shared computing resources over networks without having to own and maintain all of the underlying physical infrastructure.

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) defines cloud computing as a model that provides convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources, including networks, servers, storage, applications and services. These resources can be rapidly provided with minimal management effort (Mell & Grance, 2011).

Cloud computing is commonly associated with three service models: Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), and Software as a Service (SaaS).

**Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)** provides organisations with computing infrastructure such as virtual servers, storage and networking. Instead of purchasing physical servers, a university can obtain these resources through the cloud.

**Platform as a Service (PaaS)** provides an environment that allows developers to build, test and deploy applications without having to manage all of the underlying hardware and operating-system infrastructure.

**Software as a Service (SaaS)** allows users to access software applications through the internet rather than installing and maintaining the software locally.

These models are important in higher education because universities require different types of technology. For example, developers may use cloud platforms to build student applications, researchers may require large amounts of computing power, and students may access learning systems through internet-based applications.

### 2.2 Amazon Web Services

Amazon launched AWS in the mid-2000s, and the platform has subsequently developed into a major cloud computing provider. AWS provides a large range of services that organisations can use for computing, storage, databases, networking, analytics, artificial intelligence and machine learning.

The main advantage of this approach for a university is that it does not necessarily have to purchase all of the physical infrastructure required to support these services. Instead, resources can be provisioned when required. This can be particularly useful where demand changes significantly throughout the academic year.

### 2.3 AWS in Higher Education

Cloud adoption in higher education increased significantly from the late 2010s and accelerated during the COVID-19 pandemic. Universities were required to provide teaching, assessment and administration remotely, increasing the importance of reliable online systems.

South African universities have also adopted cloud technologies. The University of Pretoria moved its Blackboard Learning Management System and related learning analytics onto AWS in December 2018 and reported an increase in student course access following the move (Connecting Africa, 2026). The University of the Witwatersrand also moved its Sakai Learning Management System to the cloud during the COVID-19 lockdown after its on-premises system experienced difficulties under peak demand (Silicon Overdrive, n.d.).

AWS has also supported cloud skills development in South African higher education through the AWS Academy programme. Institutions including the University of Johannesburg, University of Cape Town and Durban University of Technology have participated in AWS-related education and training initiatives (BOTI, 2024; MyBroadband, 2018).

These examples demonstrate that cloud computing can be used not only to host university systems but also to develop the digital skills required by students and staff.

# 3. Benefits of AWS in South African Universities

## 3.1 Cost Efficiency

One of the major benefits of AWS is that universities can reduce the need for large upfront investments in physical servers and data-centre infrastructure. Traditional on-premises infrastructure requires an institution to purchase servers, storage equipment, networking equipment, backup systems, cooling systems and other supporting infrastructure. These resources also require maintenance and replacement over time.

AWS uses a pay-as-you-go approach, allowing institutions to pay for computing resources according to their usage. This can help universities manage their resources more efficiently, particularly when computing demand changes during the academic year.

For example, a university may experience significantly higher system usage during student registration than during academic holidays. Cloud resources can be increased during high-demand periods and reduced when demand falls. This reduces the need to permanently maintain enough physical infrastructure for the institution's highest possible workload.

However, cost efficiency depends on proper management. Cloud computing does not automatically guarantee lower costs. Universities need budgets, monitoring systems and usage policies to ensure that unnecessary resources are not left running.

## 3.2 Scalability and Reliability

AWS can provide universities with scalable computing resources. Scalability means that computing resources can be increased when demand rises and reduced when demand decreases.

This is particularly useful for university systems. For example, thousands of students may attempt to register for courses at the same time at the beginning of an academic year. Similarly, examination and results systems can experience large increases in traffic during specific periods.

If a university's infrastructure cannot handle these increases, systems can become slow or unavailable. Cloud infrastructure can help institutions provide additional resources during peak periods and reduce them afterwards.

The University of Pretoria's experience with cloud-hosted learning systems provides an example of how cloud adoption can support increased access to online learning resources (Connecting Africa, 2026).

## 3.3 Support for Remote and Hybrid Learning

AWS can support remote and hybrid education by providing cloud-hosted applications and learning platforms that students can access through the internet.

Students can use online systems to access learning materials, submit assignments, participate in online activities and communicate with lecturers without being physically present on campus.

This became particularly important during the COVID-19 pandemic, when universities were required to move significant parts of teaching and administration online. It also remains relevant for distance-learning and part-time students who may not be able to attend campus every day.

For South African universities, cloud-based systems can therefore support greater flexibility in education. However, the benefits depend on students having suitable devices, electricity and reliable internet connectivity.

## 3.4 Resilience Against Local Infrastructure Problems

Another important benefit in the South African context is resilience against certain local infrastructure problems. AWS data centres have their own infrastructure and redundancy, meaning that cloud-hosted services are not directly dependent on electricity being continuously available at a university campus.

This can be useful during periods of load shedding. If an important university application is hosted in the cloud, the application infrastructure can continue operating even when the university campus experiences a power interruption, provided that users and university network infrastructure still have internet access (Pepla Blog, 2026; Seacom, n.d.).

However, cloud computing cannot completely eliminate the effects of load shedding. Campus Wi-Fi equipment, network infrastructure, computers and students' home internet equipment can still be affected by electricity interruptions. Therefore, universities need backup power and connectivity strategies alongside cloud adoption.

## 3.5 Access to Advanced Research and Artificial Intelligence Technologies

AWS provides access to high-performance computing, data analytics, artificial intelligence and machine-learning technologies.

Universities conduct research that may require large amounts of computing power. Purchasing specialised hardware for occasional research projects can be expensive. Cloud computing allows researchers to obtain computing resources when required instead of maintaining expensive hardware permanently.

For example, a research project involving large datasets or machine-learning models may require significant processing power for a limited period. Cloud resources can be provisioned for the project and reduced when the processing requirements decrease.

This can allow universities, including institutions with limited access to specialised physical infrastructure, to participate in advanced digital research.

## 3.6 Skills Development and Graduate Employability

AWS can also contribute to the development of cloud computing skills among university students.

Through programmes such as AWS Academy and AWS re/Start, students and other participants can gain exposure to cloud technologies and industry-related skills. AWS re/Start programmes have operated in locations including Johannesburg, Pretoria and Mahikeng, with the objective of developing cloud skills and improving employment opportunities (AWS Training and Certification Blog, n.d.).

This is important because cloud computing and cybersecurity skills are increasingly relevant in the technology industry. Universities can therefore use AWS not only as infrastructure but also as part of their academic and professional skills-development programmes.

For South African students, exposure to cloud technologies can help bridge the gap between academic learning and industry requirements.

# 4. Challenges of Adopting AWS

## 4.1 Data Security and Regulatory Compliance

Universities store large quantities of personal and academic information belonging to students and staff. This may include names, contact details, academic records and other personal information.

South African universities must therefore consider the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) when using cloud services. Moving information to AWS does not transfer the university's responsibility for protecting that information. The university remains responsible for appropriate data governance, including determining where data is stored, how it is classified, how it is encrypted and who is allowed to access it (Amazon Web Services, n.d.; IBM, n.d.).

Universities must also consider requirements relating to transferring personal information outside South Africa. POPIA Section 72 places conditions on certain cross-border transfers of personal information (VDT Attorneys, n.d.).

This means that universities should conduct proper data assessments before migrating systems and should establish clear policies for access control, encryption, storage and data transfers.

## 4.2 Cost Management

Although AWS can reduce the need for large capital investments, cloud services can still become expensive if they are not properly managed.

The pay-as-you-go model means that costs can increase as usage increases. If university departments create unnecessary resources, leave services running when they are not required, or fail to monitor usage, the institution may receive unexpected costs.

Universities should therefore establish cloud governance policies and use cost-monitoring tools. Departments should also understand that cloud resources are not unlimited free resources simply because there is no physical server on campus.

## 4.3 Connectivity and Load Shedding

Cloud services depend heavily on network connectivity. Although AWS infrastructure may remain operational during load shedding, students and university staff still need electricity and internet connectivity to access the services.

In areas where broadband coverage is inconsistent, students may struggle to access online learning platforms. Power interruptions can also affect campus routers, Wi-Fi equipment, computers and other networking infrastructure.

This means that moving university systems to AWS cannot solve South Africa's connectivity challenges by itself. Universities should combine cloud adoption with reliable campus networks, backup electricity, alternative connectivity options and offline access where possible (African Times, 2025; Cambridge Management Consulting, 2025).

## 4.4 Shortage of Cloud and Cybersecurity Skills

Effective cloud adoption requires employees who understand cloud architecture, security, networking, databases and cost management.

A university may therefore face difficulties if its existing IT staff do not have sufficient cloud expertise. The institution may need to provide training or hire specialists, which creates additional costs.

South Africa has documented shortages in cloud computing and cybersecurity skills, which can make it difficult for institutions to build sufficient internal capacity (School of IT, n.d.).

Training programmes such as AWS Academy can help universities develop these skills internally. Developing internal expertise can also reduce dependence on external contractors.

## 4.5 Vendor Lock-In

Another challenge is vendor lock-in. Once a university moves large and complex systems to AWS, transferring those systems to another cloud provider may require significant time, money and technical work.

For example, if a university builds applications specifically around AWS services, moving those applications to another provider may require changes to the software and architecture.

Universities should therefore consider portability and interoperability when designing cloud systems. Important data should also be backed up appropriately so that the institution is not completely dependent on one provider.

## 4.6 Cross-Border Data Transfer

AWS is a global cloud provider, and universities must consider where their information is stored and processed.

POPIA places restrictions and requirements on certain transfers of personal information outside South Africa. Universities therefore need appropriate policies and safeguards when using international cloud infrastructure (VDT Attorneys, n.d.).

This makes data governance an important part of cloud adoption. Before moving student or staff information to a cloud environment, universities should understand where the information will be processed and ensure that the relevant legal and institutional requirements are satisfied.

# 5. Conclusion and Recommendations

Amazon Web Services can provide South African universities with access to scalable computing infrastructure, online learning services, advanced research technologies and cloud-based applications without requiring every institution to build and maintain all of the necessary physical infrastructure itself.

The analysis shows that AWS can provide several benefits, including cost efficiency, scalability, support for remote learning, improved resilience against certain local infrastructure problems, access to artificial intelligence and research technologies, and opportunities for cloud skills development. South African examples involving the University of Pretoria, University of the Witwatersrand and AWS Academy programmes demonstrate how cloud technologies can be relevant to higher education.

However, AWS adoption also presents challenges. Universities must manage data security and POPIA compliance, control cloud costs, address connectivity and electricity challenges, develop cloud and cybersecurity skills, and consider vendor lock-in and cross-border data transfers.

Based on these benefits and challenges, the following recommendations are proposed:

1. **Adopt a phased migration strategy.** Universities should begin with suitable lower-risk systems before moving highly critical administrative systems to the cloud. This allows institutions to gain experience and identify problems before expanding the migration.

2. **Establish strong data governance.** Universities should appoint appropriate information-governance personnel and establish policies covering data classification, encryption, access control, storage locations and cross-border transfers.

3. **Invest in staff and student training.** Universities should make use of programmes such as AWS Academy and AWS re/Start to develop internal cloud expertise and prepare students for technology-related employment.

4. **Implement strong cloud cost management.** Institutions should monitor cloud usage, establish budgets and create policies controlling the creation and use of cloud resources.

5. **Maintain reliable campus infrastructure.** Cloud adoption should be combined with backup electricity, reliable campus networks and alternative connectivity solutions. Cloud computing should complement rather than replace the university's broader infrastructure-resilience strategy.

6. **Plan for portability and continuity.** Universities should consider how applications and data could be moved or recovered if their requirements change. Appropriate backups and system documentation should be maintained.

Overall, AWS can form an important part of the digital transformation of South African universities, but successful adoption depends on more than simply moving systems to the cloud. Universities need a combination of appropriate technology, financial planning, cybersecurity, legal compliance, reliable connectivity and skilled personnel. A carefully planned and governed approach can allow institutions to obtain the benefits of cloud computing while reducing the associated risks.
